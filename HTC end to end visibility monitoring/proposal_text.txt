





Ethiopian Airlines' A/C Maintenance Program & Task Card Engineering (MPTC) team has successfully implemented an automated daily notification system alerting engineers whenever a new Hard Time (HT) configuration slot is created in Maintenix — resolving the visibility gap documented in RCCA No. 08-April-2026 (IT Ticket #6115802).
While this first layer of visibility addressed the slot-creation gap, two additional critical blind spots remain in the end-to-end lifecycle of Hard Time Components (HTCs). This proposal defines a systematic extension of the existing alert framework by introducing two new automated email notifications:
Alert A – HTC Part Install / Remove Alert: Real-time notification whenever an HTC part is attached to or detached from any configuration slot in Maintenix, ensuring the MP & Planning team can verify that no missing mandatory Hard Time is created for every part movement.
Alert B – HTC XXX Serial Number Alert: Real-time notification whenever an HTC part carrying the placeholder serial number 'XXX' (or equivalent unresolved S/N) is installed on a configuration slot, enabling immediate corrective action before a part with an unresolvable traceability record enters the tracked fleet.

Together, these three alert layers (the existing slot-creation alert plus the two proposed additions) establish a fully automated HTC End-to-End Visibility Monitoring framework — covering slot creation, part installation/removal events, and serial number integrity — directly supporting airworthiness compliance and maintenance program integrity across all Ethiopian Airlines fleets.
2.1 Origin – RCCA No. 08-April-2026
In March 2026, during routine engineering and task card management activities, it was identified that the MPTC team lacked full visibility of newly created Hard Time (HT) configuration slots in Maintenix. Although slots were being created in the system, there was no automated alert or trigger mechanism to notify engineers. As a result, required maintenance tasks linked to these slots were not always defined in a timely manner, creating potential gaps in compliance and airworthiness tracking.
A Root Cause & Corrective Action (RCCA) process was conducted by a team comprising Solomon Alemu (MPTC), Dawit Negash (Mgr., MPTC), Pineal Dibaba (TL, Operational Application Support), and Birhanu Mossie (Sr. Developer I). The root cause was identified as the absence of any automated notification mechanism in Maintenix for HT slot creation events. The corrective action — a daily service email to ac-mp-engg@ethiopianairlines.com — was implemented and confirmed operational by April 7, 2026.

2.2 Residual Risk — Gaps Not Addressed by the Existing Alert
The existing daily slot-creation alert ("Hard Time Configuration Slot Visibility Alert") notifies the team of new HT configuration slots but does not cover two subsequent stages in the HTC lifecycle that carry equally high airworthiness risk:

Both gaps involve real, time-critical events within Maintenix that can be detected automatically. Addressing them through the same automated email notification mechanism already proven by the slot-creation alert is the most direct and lowest-risk solution.


The current alert is sent from opsuser@ethiopianairlines.com with the subject line "Hard Time Configuration Slot Visibility Alret" [sic]. The alert report covers all aircraft and engine assembly TRK-class configuration slots across the Ethiopian Airlines fleet (B737 MAX, B767-300, A350, DHC-6, and others), reporting: Assembly Code, BOM Class, Config Slot Code, Config Slot Name, Part Group, Assigned Part, Creation Date, Revision Date, and Created/Revised User.

Upon implementation, the HTC End-to-End Visibility Monitoring framework will comprise three complementary alert layers:


7.1  Alert A — Functional Requirements
The system shall monitor all Maintenix inventory events (install/remove) on TRK-class HT configuration slots.
The alert shall be triggered independently for both installation events and removal events.
The email subject line shall clearly distinguish between INSTALL and REMOVE events using a prefix tag.
The alert shall include all data fields listed in Section 5.1.1.
The alert shall include a direct link to the relevant Maintenix inventory record for the affected aircraft.
A 'Task Defined (Y/N)' field shall indicate whether the mandatory HT task already exists in Maintenix for the receiving aircraft.
Delivery frequency shall be event-driven or, at minimum, twice-daily batch at 08:00 and 16:00 EAT.

7.2  Alert B — Functional Requirements
The system shall monitor all Maintenix installation events on TRK-class HT configuration slots and check the recorded serial number.
The alert shall trigger if the serial number field of the installed part contains 'XXX' (case-insensitive). The system shall also flag equivalent placeholders: 'UNKNOWN', 'N/A', 'NONE', or empty/null serial number fields, subject to confirmation of which placeholder values are in use within Maintenix.
The alert email shall use the subject prefix [ACTION REQUIRED] to distinguish it from informational alerts.
Alert B shall deliver immediately upon event detection, not deferred to a batch cycle.
The email shall include a calculated resolution deadline field (24 hours from event or next revenue flight, whichever is sooner).
All Alert B events shall be logged in a resolution tracking register maintained by the MPTC team.

7.3  System / Technical Requirements
Both alerts shall be dispatched from opsuser@ethiopianairlines.com, consistent with the existing slot-creation alert.
The Maintenix database queries underlying each alert shall be reviewed and approved by IT (Pineal D. / Birhanu M.) and MPTC (Solomon A.) before deployment.
The alerts shall be configurable for recipient list management without requiring a code change.
Alert delivery failures shall be logged and reported to IT Operations on a daily basis.
All alert emails shall be retained in the group mailbox (ac-mp-engg@) for a minimum of 12 months for audit purposes.


8.1  Daily Monitoring Obligations
In addition to the existing daily slot-creation alert monitoring, MPTC engineers shall:
Monitor the group mailbox (ac-mp-engg@ethiopianairlines.com) for Alert A (Install/Remove) notifications at least twice per business day.
Treat all Alert B (XXX S/N) emails as high-priority action items requiring response within 24 hours or prior to next revenue flight.
Assign fleet-specific alert follow-up to the responsible fleet engineer upon receipt.

8.3  Response Actions — Alert B (XXX S/N)
Identify the actual serial number from the work order, component release certificate (CRC/EASA Form 1 or FAA 8130-3), or shop documentation.
Update the serial number in Maintenix immediately.
Verify and activate the associated Hard Time maintenance task(s) for the resolved part.
Confirm resolution within 24 hours or before the aircraft's next revenue flight, whichever is sooner.
Log the resolution in the Alert B tracking register with: Alert date, Aircraft, Config slot, Part No., Resolved S/N, Engineer responsible, and Resolution date.
